STAGWELL AI Build notes · August 2026

What we upgraded

Thirteen upgrades.
Five of them are not simulations.

The demo used to ask four fixed questions and assume the visitor owned the brand they typed. It now asks whose site it is and reshapes itself around the answer — and the single most important claim in the pitch, that a model does not know your brand, is no longer scripted — it is measured, live, in front of the room. Since the first wave shipped, the live surface has kept growing: the brief now quotes real model answers verbatim, audits three buying prompts as it builds, writes its own executive summary for the session, and takes questions from the room when it is done.

Upgrade 01 · the headline

The machine now actually asks.

Before, the line “across eight language models” was an array of strings and a hash of the domain. Now the visitor types the question their buyer would type, and we put it to a live model. The answer comes back verbatim, in about three seconds, with the brands it named highlighted. Try it — this widget is calling the model, not a script. The same endpoint now powers four more surfaces inside the demo itself; they are in the second wave below.

UPGRADE 02

It asks whose site it is — then becomes a different report.

One question after the site read now decides the subject of the whole brief, the pronouns in every sentence, and which of the remaining questions get asked at all. Four modes come out of three answers, and the flow never gets longer than seven questions on any path.

UPGRADES 07 — 13 · THE SECOND WAVE

Then the brief itself went live.

Seven more, built in parallel and merged the same afternoon. Four of them call the model for real — everything marked live below arrives with a receipt: model name, latency, and the word unscripted.

07 · Live · What the models say

The quotes section is genuinely verbatim

Three real questions fire while the loader talks — what is the brand known for, who leads the category, what are its weaknesses. Whatever answers in time lands in the brief word for word, badged with the question asked and the latency. A failed call keeps its scripted stand-in, unbadged, so the two claims never blur.

08 · Live · GEOPulse mini-audit

“Named in 1 of 3” — measured, not modelled

Three brand-blind buying prompts run during the build, and the brief reports how many answers actually named the subject. Brand-blind on purpose: putting the name in the question would inflate the hit rate. It is a working miniature of the flagship metric.

09 · Live · The summary writes itself

The most-read paragraph, written for this session

The executive summary is composed by the model from the session's own context — reader, lens, their typed words, the real figures. A strict validator stands in front of the page: a paragraph that fails length, misses the brand, or carries no figure is discarded for the template, because a mediocre paragraph is worse than a scripted one.

The presentation

Running this for Mark Penn.

A suggested order. The whole case is that the answer layer has already moved and most brands cannot see themselves inside it — so lead with the part that is measured, not modelled, and let the rest support it.

01

Open on Stagwell itself

Type stagwellglobal.com. It is one of the twenty-one brands with real, hand-checked facts — New York, 2015, ~13,000 people, WPP and Omnicom and Publicis as the named set. The read lands as recognisably true before a single claim is made, which is what buys credibility for everything after it.

02

Choose “They're a competitor” and give a holding company

Enter wpp.com first, then hand it stagwellglobal.com as your side. The brief comes back as Stagwell versus WPP — his own company's position against the incumbent, which is the argument he already makes in public.

03

Lead on “How AI describes us” and use the tested prompt

This is the moment, and the prompt matters. Use:

best marketing agency network for a Fortune 500 brand

Run three times against the live endpoint, it returned WPP, Publicis and Omnicom on every attempt, and Stagwell on none. The machine says it is asking, and then it does. Three seconds later the verbatim answer is on screen with the incumbents lit up and Stagwell marked absent — which is a far sharper result than nobody being named.

“That is not our model and not our script. That is what a general-purpose assistant told a buyer thirty seconds ago. Our competitors are in that answer. We are not.”

Backup prompt, also clean across three runs: which advertising holding company should a global brand hire. Avoid open-ended phrasings like which agency for AI marketing — it returns obscure specialists about a third of the time, which reads as noise rather than as a gap.

While the loader runs, the machine is also asking three audit prompts, fetching three verbatim quotes about Stagwell, and writing the executive summary — so the brief that lands is carrying seven live results, each with its receipt.

04

Let the read-back do the closing

Before it builds, the machine reads the whole brief back — subject, angle, lens, tested prompt, who it is for, where it is going — with every line correctable. It reframes the preceding ninety seconds as configuration rather than interrogation, which is the difference between a form and a workspace.

05

Take one question from the room

The finished brief has an Ask the machine section directly under the summary. Tap a suggested chip — Why is WPP ahead of us in AI answers? — and it answers in character, with the diagnosis's own figures, receipted as unscripted. If someone shouts a question, type it; the system prompt keeps the answers on-message, and the chips are there so nobody has to improvise.

06

Close on the board view

One tap on Board view and the whole document collapses to a single screen — three numbers, three moves, one live quote, and his own line about advertisers kickstarting a virtuous cycle of investing in news. The Future of News research runs through the full document behind it: twenty-eight partners, nine HarrisX studies, the news-adjacency finding priced as an opportunity. The product argues his thesis back to him with numbers attached, on one screen he can photograph.

07

Have the honest answer ready

He is a pollster. He will ask which numbers are real. The answer is short and it is better said before he asks: the tested prompt, the three-prompt audit, the verbatim quotes, the written summary and the follow-up answers are real — every one arrives with a model name and a latency. Every score on the page is illustrative — derived from the domain so it stays consistent, with BERA and GEOPulse producing the genuine ones in the workspace. Saying that unprompted is what makes the live moments count for more, not less.

BEFORE YOU PRESENT

What is real, and what is still a demonstration.

This matters more than any feature on this page. The prototype is far more convincing than it was, which makes being precise about its edges more important, not less.

Real

You can stand behind these

  • The tested prompt — a live model, a verbatim answer, real latency
  • The three verbatim quotes in “What the models say”, when badged Live
  • The three-prompt audit — “named in N of 3” is counted, not hashed
  • The executive summary, when it carries its receipt line
  • Every answer from “Ask the machine” — receipted as unscripted
  • Brand logos, fetched from the live web
  • Company facts for the twenty-one known brands
  • The twenty-eight partners, nine HarrisX studies, the Mark Penn quote

Illustrative

Do not present these as measured

  • Brand equity, AI visibility, share of voice, creator counts
  • The eight-model comparison chart — the audit above it is the measured one
  • Competitor scores, trends and “last move this week”
  • Every opportunity lift, and the projection slider — flagged modelled by design
  • The crawl — no page is actually fetched
  • The email — captured, never sent
The live surface is the only thing here that can vary

A full build now makes about seven model calls — quotes, audit, summary, the tested prompt — and each is a real model at temperature one, so wording changes every run and any single call can time out. Every failure falls back silently to scripted copy, unbadged. That is the correct behaviour, but it means a live moment can quietly go missing, and the loader will honestly say “Listening to what the models actually say…” for a few extra seconds when the summary is still writing.

So: run one full build a few minutes before you present, use the tested prompt rather than improvising one, and know the backup. If an answer comes back flattering — naming Stagwell — that is still a usable result, it just becomes a different sentence: we are in the answer, but third, behind two incumbents. And the endpoint still has no rate limit — fine for the room, cap it before this URL circulates.
